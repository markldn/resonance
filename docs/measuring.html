The quick way

Record your instrument in any recorder (see below), cut it into single notes, then click From audio… under Design → Instrument (or drop the files on the page). Name the files by their key (C4, F#3, Piano.mf.Eb3, A0_v40) and Resonance places them on a piano bar; files without a name are placed by their pitch, and you can click any key to choose its file by hand. It measures every note in the browser, matches the loudness of each note to its recording and saves the instrument. The rest of this guide explains how to record well, and the command-line analyzers (tools/analyze_instrument.py for struck and plucked notes, tools/analyze_sustained.py for bowed and blown ones).

What you're making

An instrument file (resonance-instrument/1, JSON) that describes how a real instrument sounds, key by key: the level of every overtone and how fast each one dies away, at one or more playing strengths. Resonance plays it with the same physical model as every built-in instrument. All sliders (hardness, unison, impedance, size, reverb…) work on top of your measurement.

1 · What you need

  • The instrument, in tune, and a quiet room. Heavily reverberant rooms make the decays look longer.
  • One microphone (a small-diaphragm condenser is ideal) and any recorder or audio interface. WAV or FLAC, 44.1 kHz or more, 24-bit if you can.
  • Python 3 with numpy scipy soundfile: pip install numpy scipy soundfile
  • The analyzer: tools/analyze_instrument.py (in the tools/ folder of this app).

2 · Set up

  • Mic position: grand piano, lid open, 30–50 cm above the strings around the middle of the case, pointing at the soundboard. Upright: 30 cm from the open top or in front of the removed lower panel. Keep it in the same place for the whole session.
  • Level: set the gain once on the loudest note you will record (a low note, fortissimo) so the peaks reach about −6 dBFS. Never change the gain afterwards. The analyzer uses the level differences between notes and velocities.
  • Switch off fridges and fans. Close the windows. Silence between notes matters.

3 · What to record

KeysAt least every 3rd semitone (C, E♭, G♭, A in every octave). Every key is best. Add the keys just below and above the bass/tenor string break, and the lowest and highest key.
StrengthsThree per key: soft (pp), medium (mf), loud (ff). One is enough to start. The engine shapes other velocities from the difference between your layers.
DurationHold the key until the sound has completely died, or at least 15 s in the bass, 8 s mid, 3 s treble. No sustain pedal. Release the key only at the end.
One note per fileName each file with the note and the strength: C4_mf.wav, Eb2_ff.flac, A0_v40.wav (C4 = middle C; sharps as C#4, or flats as Db4). Strengths: ppp pp p mp mf f ff fff, or v<velocity> if you play from a MIDI-controlled instrument.

Tip: record one long take per strength, then cut it into single notes with any editor (Audacity: Analyze → Label Sounds, then Export Multiple). Leave about 50 ms of silence before each attack.

4 · Analyse

python3 tools/analyze_instrument.py --name "My grand" --author "Your name" \
    --license "CC-BY-4.0" --source "Neumann KM184, 40 cm over the strings, living room" \
    --out my_grand.json recordings/*.wav

For every recording it finds the attack, fits the pitch and the inharmonicity (B), measures up to 64 overtones and fits each one's two-stage decay (fast direct sound, then the slow remanent tail). It prints one line per file. Check that the reported f0 matches the note and that the bass shows many partials.

5 · Import

Click Import… under Design → Instrument and choose the JSON file. It's stored in this browser (IndexedDB), appears under Your instruments, and can be exported or deleted from Manage. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

6 · Check and refine

  • Play a slow chromatic scale. Keys that jump in loudness or colour usually come from a bad take. Re-record that key.
  • If the tails sound too long, the room was adding reverb. Record closer, or lower Board loading slightly.
  • The analyzer doesn't measure the unison detune, so use String spread to taste. It scales the typical detune for each register. To fit it per key from your recordings, run tools/calibrate_to_recordings.py (it also corrects levels and decays; see the README).
  • The file format is documented in docs/INSTRUMENT_FORMAT.md. You can also write or edit instruments by hand, for example to add detune_cents or strings per key.

Other instruments

Anything struck or plucked with a clear pitch works the same way: vibraphone, marimba, celesta, harp, guitar, electric-piano tines. Their overtones are not whole multiples of the fundamental. The analyzer measures the real ratios and the engine plays them as measured.
